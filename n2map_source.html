Two concentric cubes · configuration space

The region count of two cubes is a function of symmetry

Rotate one cube against another and count the pieces their faces cut space into. Only five answers ever occur — and which one you get is decided entirely by where the rotation axis sits inside the cube's own mirror arrangement.

The map

Every rotation is an axis together with an angle. The sphere below is the space of axes. The cube's symmetry group cuts it with nine great circles into forty-eight triangles, and that partition is the stratification: the count depends only on which piece of it your axis lands in.

Precise description

The space

Fix cube A = [−1,1]³ and let cube B be its image under a rotation R ∈ SO(3). Both share the origin, so the configuration has three degrees of freedom. In Cayley coordinates the quaternion (1, a, b, c) gives the point (a,b,c) ∈ ℝ³, which is the rotation axis scaled by tan(θ/2). The space therefore fibres over the sphere of axis directions, with the angle as fibre.

The arrangement

The octahedral group has nine mirror planes — the three coordinate planes x=0, y=0, z=0 and the six diagonal planes x=±y, y=±z, x=±z. They meet the axis sphere in nine great circles which cut it into 48 spherical triangles, the barycentric subdivision. Its vertices are the 6 face axes ⟨100⟩, the 12 edge axes ⟨110⟩ and the 8 body diagonals ⟨111⟩.

The maximum

13 is attained on the four body-diagonal circles at every angle, and on one arc of each of the six edge-axis circles. Every such circle passes through the identity. Cutting them at the cube-symmetry rotations, where the count collapses to 1, leaves a graph embedded in SO(3) — 24 vertices, and the arcs of 13 as its edges. The maximum is one-dimensional: a curve, not a region.

Small integer quaternions are the arithmetically special ones, so they sit on walls far more often than chance. That bias is a good instrument for finding rational walls and a trap for judging what is generic — an earlier reading of this same data, taken at small heights, wrongly concluded that the maximum was attained on an open set.

Two caveats

  • The angle carries its own walls. A generic axis gave 5 at one angle out of 33, so "special angle" is a codimension-1 condition independent of the axis arrangement. The count-5 locus therefore has components of two kinds — mirror axis with generic angle, and generic axis with special angle — both two-dimensional. Symmetry explains the axis direction completely; it does not explain the angle.
  • Coincidence is not monotone. The count rises as the stratum narrows — 4, then 5 or 9, then 13 — and then collapses to 1 at the most special points of all, where the two cubes become the same cube. More symmetry helps until it doesn't.
Every count exact: integer quaternions, rational arithmetic, no floating point in any decision, each configuration agreed by two independently written engines. Counts by depth are {d₁, 1} with d₁ ∈ {0, 3, 4, 8, 12}.
