Two different shapes, which must not be conflated

Every walls figure on this page counts coincidence conditions, and those do not bound the count by themselves: of the coincidence walls crossed on one ray at 727, 8 of 21 change the count and 13 do not, and the count also changes where no coincidence wall is crossed. What changes it there is not known.

A record sits in two regions at once, and they have different dimensions. Confusing them is the single easiest mistake to make here.

Coincidence variety. Configurations holding the same tight conditions: where the walls through a record meet.
A surviving direction. A curve on which every coincidence is kept. The count may or may not be kept along it; at n = 7 it is not.
Count plateau. Configurations with the same region count, a different object with different boundaries, and the one the spine's nodes describe. Its dimension is 0, 1, 1, 2, 3 at n = 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, equal to the tangent dimension everywhere except n = 3 (P381–P383); at n = 5, 9 and 10 only the tangent is known.

Two structures worth drawing

The 727 node explains why the tower has a single trunk; the n = 9 fork explains why it stops being a chain.

The 727 node: its extents, and what stands over it

n = 5 base · n = 6 node, both branches to scale · n = 7 over the record

Two branches cross at the record (P391). Through the 727 record pass exactly two arcs of equally good configurations, D1 and D2, really 4.51° apart (drawn wider). Each holds 727 on its own; no combination of them does. The arc catalogue's B and C are these same two branches, carried by the 393 base's own 120° symmetry to the record's other two spellings: B is D1 and C is D2, end for end, their solved extents coinciding exactly (P392). Arc A is a separate piece and meets no spelling of the record.

D1 is drawn to scale in its parameter. The 727 plateau runs from t = −2/19, exactly rational, to 0.196487974607…, a root of 1007x² − 21390x + 4164: the first plateau boundary carried all the way from a count bracket to a wall to a polynomial to a root. The pale bar is the swept window that was on file before, (−1/8, 1/4): wider at both ends, and the 713 point at t = 2/9 lies inside it but outside the plateau.

D2 is drawn to the same scale in its own parameter. Its plateau, (−0.13578, 0.22073), was solved from arc C's ends and checked with the exact engine on both sides of each. It is punctured at t = −3/31, where the count is 723: arc C's special point at s = 164/87, seen from the record.

Special points. Nine special points on the catalogue's four arcs are six compounds (P294). In the node's own terms: D1 carries a 725 at its lower end (the same compound as arc B's lower end), the record, and the 713 outside; D2 carries the record and the 723 puncture; arc A carries two 725s found nowhere else.

At n = 7 the crossing is carried up one dimension (P393, P394). The 1217's first six cubes are exactly the record, and its seventh cube's coordinates are the fibre above it. Adding that seventh cube, 4,−3,−4,−4, to a point of either branch gives 1217 along a segment (the gold bars): on D1 from its lower wall, −2/19, up to ≈0.0497; on D2 from its lower end, −0.1358, up to ≈0.0702. Over each segment the seventh cube can also slide along the fibre, which sweeps the segment out into a sheet (the top of the figure). Both sheets contain the fibre segment over the record, from u = −0.0453 to +0.00255, and each extends to both sides of it, so the two sheets cross along that segment, as the two branches cross at the record. The record's own 1217 is one point on the crossing. Beyond the bars, and everywhere on arc A, this seventh cube gives 1213 or fewer; other seventh cubes have not been tried there. (The 1217s found over the record's other spellings, 7,7,9,7 and 15,1,1,−1, are the same 1217 respelled.)

Reading the figure. A filled dot is a configuration at its level's record count; a hollow dot is an interruption, where the count differs. The thick strokes are 727 extents; the gold bars are the segments that lift to 1217, a different predicate over each branch's parameter, so they run beside the branches and not on them. The pale stroke is the old swept figure. Arc A is on its own scale and is dashed because nothing connects it to the node. Detail: the two 1217 sheets, measured

n = 7 · each sheet in its own plane · three walls · solved

Each sheet is a curved triangle. Its left edge is its branch's lower 727 wall; its top is a flat fibre wall at u = 0.00255, the same on both sheets; and its whole lower-right side is one curved wall, of total degree 4, found first on the D1 sheet (P301). Its exact root matches the measured boundary on 33 of 34 measured lines; the 34th stopped on a seam, and direct counts put it on the wall too. The corners are its roots on the other two walls. The gold bar at t = 0 is the crossing: the same fibre segment lies in both sheets.

Seams. Two dotted lines cross the sheets with 1217 on both sides and a lower count on the line itself: u = −1/36 on both (1215), and t = −3/31 on D2 (1213), which is the n = 6 723 puncture, lifted.

This replaces a pentagon (P394). The earlier panel came from a 5×5 grid spanning the measured extents at the axes, and drew the grid's bottom row and right column as edges. Neither is a wall: the sheet continues below that row, to u = −0.11, and past that column. The curved wall and its solution stand; the region they were drawn around was the grid's box.

Not measured: anything outside each sheet's own plane of fibre and branch.

The n = 9 fork — one count, two compounds

identical at every depth · not interchangeable

Both count 2787 with the same depth profile at all nine depths, and were treated as one configuration. They are not: 396 vs 420 tight conditions, 76 vs 83 walls, lineality 5 vs 4. Simplifying the representative moved the point, and the n = 10 chain broke with it.

What this does not show. The min-feature row is the closest pair of arrangement vertices lying on two-cube intersection curves; a vertex off those curves is not enumerated, so a smaller feature could exist. The “free dirs” column counts isolated directions in a projective space — it is not a dimension, and the sequence 0, 0, 0, 1, 2, 11 has no known law: a fitted one was predicted and refuted. The n = 6 and n = 7 entries were recomputed after a defect was found in the polynomial used to test them.

Instrument scope, named rather than implied. "Last-cube tangents" counts directions in the last cube's slice, which is all `tangents_eps` examines; probing base directions found the plateau is ≥2-dimensional at n = 7 and n = 8. "Free dirs" counts isolated points of a projective space, not a dimension. "Min feature" covers vertices on two-cube intersection curves only. Six such restrictions are catalogued in the project's gap register.

Every number here is exact rational or field arithmetic — no floating point, no sampling except where a bracket is explicitly stated.
