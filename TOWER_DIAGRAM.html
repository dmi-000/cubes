Two different shapes, which must not be conflated

A record sits in two regions at once, and they have different dimensions. Confusing them is the single easiest mistake to make here.

Coincidence variety. Configurations holding the same tight conditions. Walls meet at a point — nothing can move.
A surviving direction. From n = 7 the variety is a curve: coincidences are preserved along it, the count is not.
Count plateau. Configurations with the same region count. A different object with different boundaries. The published 0, 0, 2, 1, 1 at n = 4…8 counts last-cube tangents only — the plateau is ≥2-dimensional at n = 7 and n = 8, and n = 4–6 are unresolved rather than zero.

Two structures worth drawing

The 727 node explains why the tower has a single trunk; the n = 9 fork explains why it stops being a chain.

The 727 node: its extents, and what stands over it

n = 5 base · n = 6 arcs, arc D to scale in s · n = 7 fibres

Nine special points across the four arcs collapse to six compounds under a constructive congruence test. Ringed points are the record's class, joined by the dashed tie: arcs D, B and C each carry a different configuration congruent to it — at s = 0, 16/35 and 132/29 respectively — and arc A carries none. The tie is a congruence, not a coincidence of parameter: the three are drawn at one height for legibility, and since arc D's height is metric while B's and C's are not, each point's own s is printed beneath its arc.

Arc D is drawn to scale, so its three extents can be read off directly. The 727 plateau runs from s = −2/19, exactly rational, to 0.196487974607… — a quadratic irrational, root of 1007x² − 21390x + 4164. The 1217 plateau nests inside it, sharing the lower wall and ending about four times earlier. The pale bar is the figure that was on file: it is wider at both ends, and the 713 special point at s = 2/9 sits inside it but outside the plateau, counting 713 rather than 727.

The chain closes here for the first time. Count-stepping brackets the upper end in (179/911, 56/285); the wall keys name the two conditions — a ± pair — whose polynomials have a root there; restricted to the arc's line that polynomial is the quadratic above; its root lies inside the bracket that found it. The solve had missed this end because it is irrational and the search looks for rational roots — it was one of the ten the run reported as "irrational, not testable". Arc D's other crossing tangent is still swept.

The 1217 plateau is one object, drawn once — and it really is a surface. The gold patch at the record is spanned by two transverse directions, one inside the fibre and one along the base (the arc D lift). Only one of the two gold elements is a projection: the patch is the plateau, the bar beside arc D is its shadow two layers down, whose extent (−2/19, ≈0.0497) is a subset of the 727 extent (−2/19, ≈0.1965) beside it, sharing the lower wall.

Straight means a direction; curved means a correspondence. The dotted links to the 727 dots are drawn as curves because nothing travels along them — and because straightened they ran at almost exactly the fibre axes' slope, close enough that a fibre axis's endpoint landed on one, so the pair read as a single line that inexplicably faded. That faint point meant nothing; it was an artifact of where I stopped a stroke.

A gold line is a motion; the dotted line is not. The faded axis through the 1217 dot is the fibre — the 3-dimensional space of seven-cube configurations sharing that 727 — and a motion can lie in it, which is what the gold in-fibre direction does. The dotted line to the 727 dot is the addition of the seventh cube: a correspondence between a point of ambient 15 and a point of ambient 18, so nothing can lie along it and it is drawn so that nothing looks as though it does. An earlier version ran a single solid line from the 727 dot to the 1217 dot, fusing the two and inviting exactly the question of what a motion along it would mean.

Both strokes are directions at the record, so both pass through the 1217 dot and their crossing is that dot — a crossing anywhere else would assert a second distinguished point that does not exist. Each is drawn asymmetric about the record in the ratio its brackets give: about 23 : 1 along the fibre, about 2 : 1 along the base.

That the patch is filled was checked, not assumed — and it was drawn before it was checked. Every combination of the two directions holds the count (`fib±base`, `fib+2base`, `2fib+base`, out to t = 1/16384), so the plateau is a genuine 2-dimensional surface. The analogous case at n = 6 is the opposite: two directions hold and no combination does, making that a node — two arcs crossing, not a surface. Not measured: the surface's extent in combination directions. The parallelogram says "2-dimensional here", not "this shape", and its two edges belong to different parts of the ambient space, so no single layer of this diagram contains it.

Reading the figure. A filled dot is a configuration counting its level's record value; a hollow dot is an interruption, where the count differs — so the hollow 725, 723 and 713 points are breaks in the 727 plateau rather than objects sitting on it. The dashed tie means congruence. The thick teal stroke on arc D is the 727 extent, a property of six-cube configurations; the gold stroke beside it is the 1217 shadow, a property of those same configurations once extended by the fixed seventh cube. Two different predicates over one parameter, so they run parallel — an earlier version drew them concentric, which implied they were the same kind of claim. The pale stroke is the swept figure, which is a claim about the 727 extent and is drawn on the arc with it.

The gold stroke and the gold dot are one object. The 1217 plateau is at least 2-dimensional: it runs one way inside the fibre — bracketed at (1/512, 1/266) upward and (4/89, 1/22) downward, about 23× longer down than up — and the other way along arc D itself, which is the bar. The bar is the plateau's shadow on the arc, not a second thing; the dashed line is the projection. That the plateau has a base direction at all was invisible until it was looked for: the instrument that reported "one tangent" at n = 7 searches the last-cube slice only.

1217 is above the picture, not on it. Its first six cubes are exactly the record, so it projects onto arc D's ringed point, and the seventh cube's three coordinates are the fibre over it. The upper dashed tie is the lower one lifted: applying each witnessing rotation to 1217's seventh cube gives 7,7,9,7 over arc B and 15,1,1,−1 over arc C, both counting 1217 and both congruent to it. Fibres fade rather than stop — a fibre has no end.

The n = 9 fork — one count, two compounds

identical at every depth · not interchangeable

Both count 2787 with the same depth profile at all nine depths, and were treated as one configuration. They are not: 396 vs 420 tight conditions, 76 vs 83 walls, lineality 5 vs 4. Simplifying the representative moved the point, and the n = 10 chain broke with it.

What this does not show. The min-feature row is the closest pair of arrangement vertices lying on two-cube intersection curves; a vertex off those curves is not enumerated, so a smaller feature could exist. The “free dirs” column counts isolated directions in a projective space — it is not a dimension, and the sequence 0, 0, 0, 1, 2, 11 has no known law: a fitted one was predicted and refuted. The n = 6 and n = 7 entries were recomputed after a defect was found in the polynomial used to test them.

Instrument scope, named rather than implied. "Last-cube tangents" counts directions in the last cube's slice, which is all `tangents_eps` examines; probing base directions found the plateau is ≥2-dimensional at n = 7 and n = 8. "Free dirs" counts isolated points of a projective space, not a dimension. "Min feature" covers vertices on two-cube intersection curves only. Six such restrictions are catalogued in the project's gap register.

Every number here is exact rational or field arithmetic — no floating point, no sampling except where a bracket is explicitly stated.
