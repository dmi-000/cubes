The 183 Plateau

The n = 4 record is not an isolated point. It lies on an arc of length 0.687 along which every region count stays 183 — and the second 183 class lies on a different arc, which a Lipschitz bound settles without extrapolation.

Distance to the second 183 class, along the record's arc

Horizontal axis: arc length from the record, forward to the left, backward to the right. Vertical axis: the gauge-free distance to the second class — each cube compared as a coset, minimised over the octahedral group, relabelling, and the residual global rotation. Reaching the second class means touching zero. The arc ends before either branch can.

Why this is a proof and not an extrapolation. Distance to a fixed point is 1-Lipschitz in arc length: moving a distance s along the curve can reduce it by at most s.

Forward. The whole branch is 0.0517 long, so every point on it lies within 0.0517 of the record. The second class is at 0.06504. It cannot be there.

Backward. At arc 0.60 the distance is 0.05072, with 0.0356 of arc left. Closing that gap needs slope 1.42 > 1.

So the two 183 classes lie on two distinct maximal constant-count arcs. What this does not show: that the 183 locus is globally disconnected — a link through components of a different contact type is untouched.

The arc itself

Count 183 holds along the whole length. The record sits close to one end, not in the middle — and the two ends fail in different ways.
